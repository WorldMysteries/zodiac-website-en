A symbol instead of a name

The crossed circle is a gunsight and an astrological sign at once. It appeared on the letters, on the chest of the costume at Lake Berryessa and on the victims’ car door. It was not a signature but a brand.

Theater for the press

The killer demanded that the ciphers be printed on the front pages and threatened to kill “twelve people over the weekend”. He phoned the police, dictated details and enjoyed being hunted by the entire state.

✕ — none of the traces matched any known suspect.

Why it matters today

In December 2020 the Z340 cipher was cracked by an international team of enthusiasts — 51 years later. The case proved that an archive can speak again when people with computational methods take it up.

Chronology · 1966 — 2021

Eleven months
that brought a halt to a whole state

The confirmed attacks fit into less than a single year. Everything else is letters, phone calls, threats and decades of silence. Below is the factual sequence that investigators cross-checked across four jurisdictions.

October 30, 1966 · Riverside, California

Cheri Jo Bates — a disputed prologue

An 18-year-old student was killed by the college library: the lights had been cut, and at the scene lay a man’s watch with a stopped mechanism, plus a message on the wall. In 1967 the town received a “Confession” letter signed with initials. Police officer Serge Merrill and the press suggested a link to Zodiac; most researchers consider it unproven.

Link disputedWatch stopped“Confession” letter
December 20, 1968 · Lake Herman Road, Benicia

The first confirmed scene

David Faraday (16) and Betty Lou Jensen (16) were shot on a deserted road by Lake Herman. The weapon was a pistol; the shell casings were neatly lined up. The Solano County police spent a long time on domestic theories: there were no witnesses and almost no evidence left.

Killed: 2.22 pistol (presumed)No witnesses
Evidence: shell casings, footwear traces. The link to Zodiac was established only after his letter in July 1969.
July 4, 1969 · Blue Rock Springs Park, Vallejo

Darlene Ferrin and Mike Mageau

In the parking lot by the park an unknown man walked up to the car with a flashlight and opened fire. Darlene Ferrin (22) died; Mike Mageau (19) survived five gunshot wounds and later gave the first description of the shooter: a white male, 25–30 years old, heavyset.

Killed: 1Survived: 1First description
Forty minutes later a man phoned the Vallejo police: “I want to report a murder… I was the killer”. The caller gave details only the killer could know.
July 31 — August 4, 1969 · three newspaper offices

A cipher on the front page

Three identical letters arrived at the Vallejo Times-Herald, the San Francisco Chronicle and the San Francisco Examiner. Each opened with “This is the Zodiac speaking” and contained one third of the 408-character cipher. The condition: print it in full, or “twelve people over the weekend”.

Z-408Ultimatum to the pressPapers printed it
August 8, 1969: schoolteacher Donald Harden and his wife Betty read the text — 8 days before the official announcement.
September 27, 1969 · Lake Berryessa, Napa County

The man in the executioner’s hood

Cecelia Shepard (22) and Bryan Hartnell (20) were resting on the shore. A man in a black hooded costume with a symbol on his chest, wearing glasses, approached, introduced himself as “a policeman from Napa County” and claimed the previous murders. After tying the students up he stabbed them repeatedly. Hartnell survived; Shepard died two days later.

Killed: 1Survived: 1Costume and hood
On the car door the attacker left dates and words in black marker: “Vallejo 12-20-68, 7-4-69, Sept 27-69-6:30 by knife”. An hour later he called the Napa sheriff’s office.
October 11, 1969 · Presidio Heights, San Francisco

Cab driver Paul Stine

The 29-year-old cab driver Paul Stine was shot in his cab at the corner of Washington Street and Cherry Street. The killer took the money and cut out a piece of his blood-stained shirt — pieces of the fabric later arrived at the Chronicle newsroom as “proof”.

Killed: 1Shirt fragmentPrints on the cab
Two patrol officers drove past the cab that same minute, but they were looking for a “black man” — the dispatch from the control room had been relayed with an error. The palm print on the cab window has never matched any suspect.
October — December 1969 · letters, calls, threats

A punch card with thirteen holes

After Stine’s murder the flow of letters intensified: threats against a school bus, demands to print the ciphers, parodies, “greeting” cards with punched holes — a counter of “slaves for the afterlife”. On November 8 a new large cipher arrives at the Chronicle — Z340.

1970 · a map, radians and “My name is …”

A year of puzzles

April 20 — a letter with the thirteen-character “My name is …” cipher and a bomb claim. June 26 — a Mount Diablo map with a question about radians and the small 32-character cipher (Z32). July 24 — the “Red Phantom” letter, in which the author refers to a kidnapping that, as it turned out, never happened.

Z-13Z-32Mount DiabloRadians
The “radians + inches” puzzle still has dozens of amateur solutions pointing to different spots on the map. None has been accepted as official.
1971 — 1974 · fading out

The last universally accepted letters

March 13, 1971 — a letter to the Los Angeles Times claiming more victims (the murders of Kathy Snozey and Deborah Furlong in Santa Clara County — later linked to other people). January 29, 1974 — a letter quoting “The Exorcist” with the tally “Mea = 37, SFPD = 0”. After that the correspondence stops; certain later messages are considered forgeries.

1990 — 2002 · forensic exams

Identification and DNA

In 1990 Mike Mageau picked Arthur Leigh Allen in a photo identification — but with hesitation, and his 1969 description did not match Allen’s appearance. In 2002 a partial DNA profile was obtained from envelope glue: it did not match Allen, yet the sample is too incomplete to exclude or to name anyone.

December 3 — 11, 2020 · Z340 deciphered

Three people and 51 years of patience

The American David Oranchak, the Belgian Jarl Van Eycke and the Australian mathematician Sam Blake solved the 340-character cipher, using a genetic algorithm and the knowledge that the author had applied transpositions with a diagonal offset. On December 11 the FBI publicly confirmed the solution.

SolvedDiagonal offsetsConfirmed by the FBI
No name turned out to be in the text — only mockery of the police and television. Zodiac’s name remains unknown.
October 2021 — present

“The Case Breakers” and the official position

A group of journalists and former investigators announced that the Zodiac had been Gary Francis Poste (died 2018). The San Francisco Police Department stated that it does not confirm this theory and that the case remains open and active. No charges have been filed against anyone.

01 · Jurisdictions

Four agencies, one archive — kept apart

Benicia (Solano County), Vallejo, Napa and San Francisco each handled the cases separately. The link between the scenes was acknowledged only after the letters — by then the traces had already been dispersed among different laboratories.

02 · Prints

A palm print that belongs to no one

A clear palm print was left on the window of Stine’s cab. It has been compared against hundreds of candidates, including the prime suspect — there is still no match.

03 · DNA

A profile, but no name

In 2002 a partial DNA profile was obtained from the glue on the envelopes. It did not match Arthur Leigh Allen, but the sample is so incomplete that it excludes no one and names no one.

04 · Handwriting

Experts who disagreed

Some document examiners linked Allen’s handwriting to the letters; others did not. The block letters and the deliberate misspellings make handwriting analysis weak evidence.

05 · Testimony

A description that kept changing

The survivor Mike Mageau described the 1969 shooter as a heavyset man of 25–30. In the 1990 identification he picked Allen, but with hesitation — courts treat such testimony with caution.

06 · Claims

37 victims, 5 of them real

The author of the letters attributed dozens of murders to himself. Some of the claims were never substantiated, and every “confession” has to be verified anew — the noise drowns the signal.

07 · Copycats

Thousands of fake letters

After 1969 a flood of imitations and hoaxes poured into the newsrooms. Only the stamps, the glue and the specific scene details known to a narrow circle helped separate an original from a copy.

08 · Time

Witnesses pass away

Inspectors, reporters, survivors and suspects die; archives are moved, ledgers are destroyed. Every lost box is one less possible lead.

“We do not know whether he is alive, whether he is dead, or whether there was ever just one of him.”

Cultural footprint

How an unsolved case
became a genre

Zodiac influenced cinema more than he influenced the investigation: he created the image of “the clever killer who plays games with the police”. More important is something else — for decades amateur cryptographers and journalists kept working with the archive, and in 2020 that work paid off.

2007 · dir. David Fincher

“Zodiac”

A film based on the books by the cartoonist Robert Graysmith and on the case files brought mass attention back to the case. Fincher spent years checking every scene against the archives and deliberately refused to give an “answer” — just as the investigation never did.

1971 · dir. Don Siegel

“Dirty Harry”

The “Scorpio” killer is an almost direct cast of Zodiac: letters to the press, threats, games with the police. The film came out while the investigation was still under way.

Documentaries

Series and podcasts

“This Is the Zodiac Speaking” (History, 2014), “The Most Dangerous Animal of All” (FX, 2020) and dozens of podcasts keep the case in the public eye — and regularly generate new unverified theories.

Open data

Enthusiast archives

Sites such as zodiackiller.com (Mike Morford) and zodiacciphers.com (David Oranchak) gathered scans of the letters, envelopes and expert analyses into a single database. It was exactly this database that made the Z340 crack possible.

Ethics

The line between interest and myth

Any mention of a name in the media is a risk to living people and their families. Every suspect on this page was never convicted, and some of the theories were rebutted by the police themselves.
